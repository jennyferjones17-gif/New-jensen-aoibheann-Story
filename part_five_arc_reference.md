## [KIDS]: The Counsellor Roadmap

### The Sequence (Ideal)
- **Phase 1 (May):** Stabilise. No introduction. Consistent presence. JJ is watching: "Is this stable or still shifting?"
- **Phase 2 (Late May/Early June):** Prepare. He tells kids he's seeing someone. NOT a surprise — they already know her name. This replaces the internet's version with his.
- **Phase 3 (June, before school breaks ~June 21):** First introduction. Short (1–2 hours), neutral setting, no forced bonding, no family framing. Low-stakes, observational, optional.
- **Phase 4 (Summer, June 21–Aug 16):** Mostly separate. Parent–child time. Light integration ONLY if Phase 3 went well and no resistance.
- **Phase 5 (Before Aug 16):** He leaves for filming. Kids' experience: "We met her, nothing bad happened, and we had time with our parent after." NOT: "We met her and then Dad left."

### What Can Go Wrong
- **Kids say no.** JJ especially. She pushed back on Danneel's correction about "bad person." She's 13, has done her own research from the internet, has formed her own opinion, and rejected her mother's defence. The counsellor can recommend it. Jensen can want it. Danneel can agree. But JJ has a vote and she's already cast it.
- **Introduction goes badly.** JJ walks in already decided. Arrow asked "why do people say she's bad." Even a perfectly managed two hours can land wrong with a child who's already formed an opinion.
- **Summer distraction.** Pre-production starts Aug 11. Showrunner calls, script meetings, casting bleed into July. If he's on the phone during a beach day or cuts a dinner short, they don't blame work — they blame her. She's the new variable in their lives.
- **Overhearing.** Danneel on the phone to Gen or Hilarie about the financial negotiation. Jensen and Danneel arguing about settlement terms in the kitchen after bedtime. JJ not asleep. A comment about Aoibhéann not meant to be heard, filtered through a 13-year-old's understanding.
- **The cruel version:** Everything goes perfectly, introduction lands well, summer is stable — and then he leaves for five months and the kids are left with Danneel and whatever they're feeling about the woman Dad introduced right before he disappeared.

Do NOT introduce if: co-parent conflict is high, kids are still highly unsettled, the relationship itself is unstable. Because timing won't save it.

### The Blunt Reality
This isn't about WHEN they meet her. It's about what emotional state they are in when they meet her, and what happens immediately after.

### BUT — All Three Conditions Must Be Met
Kids need to be ready, Danneel to agree, and Aoibhéann actually being around. All three simultaneously.

## [PR STRATEGY]: The Shift

The strategy running since March 2026 (silence, containment, no confirmation) starts breaking from multiple directions:

Once kids know Dad is seeing someone, they know something the public doesn't have confirmed. JJ is 13 with a phone. Her friends have phones. Are the kids told not to tell anyone? ### From exhaustion
Over a year of hiding. Dinner companions approved, seating managed, routes around exes. He's answered the same deflection in every interview. At some point the cost of hiding exceeds the cost of being known.

### From logic
If kids are meeting her, if she's spending time on the East Coast, if the second place is real — maintaining the fiction requires absurd operational security. A neighbour in Larchmont. A school parent. A cashier.

### From accident
Interview slip. Spotted somewhere the cover photos can't explain.

### The Shift Itself Is a Decision
Do they get ahead of it, or does it happen to them?

### What Triggers the Conversation
Either: they literally realise it's unsustainable to keep hiding (especially once the kids know details the public doesn't — are they meant to keep things secret from their friends, ask their friends not to tell anyone). Or: they simply had enough and want a new strategy. Or: the slip forces it. Or: they are spotted.
