## Part Three Operating Principle

Part One had scaffolding — the charity gave them reasons to be together. Part Two had deliberate contact — every meeting a sacrifice, every call a choice. Part Three starts with silence. The absence of contact after months of it. The phone not ringing is as loud as the phone ringing.

Timeline: Mid-October 2026 onward.

### Beat 1 — The Canyon House
*Her POV — Oct 14–16*

The Canyon house. She's in it and it has him in it. Three visits — the August week, the mornings, the evenings. The kitchen where he took the Danneel call. The deck where they had the conversation. The rooms she built to be entirely hers and which now contain someone who isn't there anymore.

Cassidy comes by. Not a social call — she has the summary. The filing is public. Fan forums have built a timeline — July photos, April coffee, the filing date. Speculation growing but contained to fan spaces. Comparisons to Danneel starting in threads. Not mainstream yet.

Cassidy delivers it the way Cassidy delivers things — clear, practical, no editorialising. She's been with Aoibhéann long enough to know what she needs is information, not comfort.

Aoibhéann listens. Doesn't say much. Asks Cassidy to get her a list of interior designers.

*Los Angeles, 2021. A house she doesn't live in anymore. Movers in the hallway. The master bedroom half-empty — boxes labelled COSMO stacked in one corner, boxes labelled AOIBHÉANN in the other. Declan sitting on the stripped bed. Cassidy in the doorway with her phone and a clipboard. The specific logistics of taking a life apart and sorting it into two piles.*

### Beat 2 — Cameron's House
*Her POV — Oct 17–18*

Cameron's house. She tells her what happened on the call. What he said — that what he was offering wasn't enough. What she said back. Cameron listens. Doesn't say I told you so. Asks one question — did he say why it wasn't enough? Aoibhéann says he could see what it was costing her. Cameron sits with that. Says something like — that doesn't sound like a man who wanted to leave.

Aoibhéann doesn't engage with that. Moves on.

Cameron asks about the fan situation. Has she looked at it. Aoibhéann says Cassidy briefed her. It's fan forums — the same machinery she's dealt with since she was twenty-three. Different audience this time but the mechanics are familiar.

Cameron asks if she's okay. Aoibhéann says she's handled worse.

Cameron doesn't push further. She can see the breakup is the thing doing the damage, not the fan threads.

The brand idea surfaces. Something she's been circling since the Armani meeting in August. Her own line. Cameron hears it alongside the breakup and understands it's the same impulse — building something she can control.

Cameron asks what kind of line. Aoibhéann says she's been thinking about jewellery. Something small, permanent, quality over branding. Cameron asks the hard questions — market, margins, scale. Aoibhéann hears herself defending it on passion rather than business. Lands on beauty instead without fully deciding. Skincare-infused makeup. The thing she actually knows. The thing she's been the face of for fifteen years. Cameron says that sounds more like a business and less like a feeling. Aoibhéann hears the distinction and files it.

### Beat 2a — The Drive Home
*Her POV — same evening*

Driving back from Cameron's. Calls Declan. Tells him about the redecorating — casual, the house needs refreshing. Declan hears it. He knows the pattern. Says something about how she just got the place how she wanted it. She says people change.

### Beat 3 — NYC
*His POV — Oct 20–21*

He's in Manhattan for a meeting. Tuesday. Walking somewhere or in a car. He opens his phone and she's there — a story or a post. Dinner with friends, a restaurant, a location tag. NYC.

She's twenty blocks away. Maybe less. He could text. He could call. He could walk into the restaurant.

He checks her grid. Recent posts — the professional content her team has been pushing. Brand commitments, a shoot, the charity. The version of her life that continues without him in it. He scrolls further than he needs to. Catches himself doing it. Puts the phone away.

She doesn't know he's in the city. He has a meeting Thursday too — same week, same proximity. His team would call this a hard avoidance situation — he cannot be seen renewing contact while the continuity rumours are active. Any sighting now confirms the relationship is ongoing. Her team running the same calculation from the other side: maintain independence, no visible link to Jensen. Hard avoidance — no overlap sightings, controlled entry and exit points. Two days in her city without her knowing. He goes back to Connecticut carrying the specific weight of having been that close and choosing not to close the distance.

### Beat 4 — The Twins
*His POV — late Oct, maybe Oct 25–26*

Home from meetings. The house. The routine that has been the routine for a year — breakfast, school run, afternoons, dinner. Danneel in her orbit, him in his, the kids moving between them in the same house.

One of the twins says something. Not a question — an assumption. Something that treats the family as intact in present tense. Maybe asking about Christmas plans as though nothing has changed. Maybe referencing Mom and Dad in a way that has no separation in it. Said casually, the way eight-year-olds say things — without knowing the weight of what they've just revealed.

Jensen hears it. The twin has forgotten. Or never really absorbed it. Because nothing in their daily life has confirmed that their parents separated. Dad still makes breakfast. Dad still does the school run. Both parents at soccer. Both parents at dinner. The word "separated" was said in January and the world kept turning in exactly the same direction.

He told himself he was protecting them. Keeping everything stable while they adjusted. A year later they haven't adjusted because there's been nothing to adjust to. The protection has become its own kind of dishonesty — his kids are living in a version of their family that doesn't exist anymore and he's the one who built it.

He sits with that through Halloween. Carving pumpkins with children who think their parents are together because he made sure nothing in their lives ever suggested otherwise.

His team registers this as an internal alarm point. The family optics are becoming structurally dangerous. The priority shifts harder toward stability, parenting image, separation clarity. This is where long-term PR starts depending on real-life choices, not spin.

### Beat 5 — The Melton Photos
*Her POV — late Oct*

She's going out more. Not performing recovery — just needing to not be in the Canyon house with the interior designers and the rooms that still have him in them regardless of the new paint. Dinners, events, the social circuit she usually manages efficiently and leaves early from. She's staying later. Not drinking more — just present longer. Needing the noise.

A brand dinner. Seated next to Charles Melton. Not her choice — placement never is. He's good company, easy conversation, attractive in the way her world is full of attractive men. Nothing. She's aware of him the way she's aware of anyone seated next to her at a dinner. Polite, warm, professional.

Photos from the dinner surface. Her and Melton side by side. The press has the angle it wants.

Then Miles and Keleigh's party. Private. Their house. The kind of evening where she can actually relax — her people, no press, no performance. Melton is there because he moves in the same circles. They talk. They don't leave together. The party ends and people leave at the same time because that's how parties end.

A photo leaks. Her and Melton near the door at the same time. The framing does what framing always does — two photos, same man, the narrative writes itself.

She sees it. Handles it the way she's handled this since she was twenty-three. Keleigh is furious — someone at her party, in her home, took a photo and sold it. The violation is personal for Keleigh in a way it isn't for Aoibhéann, who has been dealing with this for over a decade.

The fan communities fold it in immediately. The Melton photos dropped into the same threads running the Jensen timeline. The same people who called her a homewrecker two weeks ago now calling her heartless — she destroyed a family and moved on inside a month. Both accusations running side by side. She broke up the marriage. She was never serious. She's a serial dater who collects men. She's a calculated social climber who targeted a married man. The contradictions don't slow anyone down.

Her team sees the Melton photos two ways. The Jensen redirect has short-term value — if the press is writing about Melton they're not writing about Jensen. But the accumulation is doing exactly what they've spent years trying to bury. The gossip blog version of her is being rebuilt in real time.

Her team's position: maintain fan-level containment. No engagement. Soft containment — avoid amplifying old narratives, keep focus on current work. Internal priority: protect contracts, protect brand perception.

### Beat 6 — Chace
*His POV — late Oct, NYCC*

New York Comic Con. Jensen and Chace in the same spaces all day — panels, press, the Boys franchise machinery. Chace is professional, present, but the warmth from the festival isn't there. Not hostile. Just cooler. Jensen notices.

Green room. Between panels. Chace venting about the week. The internet. The fan forums building the timeline from the filing and July. The Melton photos that are completely fabricated. The comparisons to Danneel getting personal in fan spaces. He's angry. Not performing it — actually angry. She's his friend. Kelsey is her friend. He was there through the gossip blog years. He watched her rebuild her image after Alex. Watched her do it again after the tabloid cycle in her mid-twenties. Watched her put in years of work to be taken seriously — the charity, the brand partnerships, the shift from party girl to someone the industry respects. And now strangers in fan forums are calling her the reason a family broke apart and she can't say a word to defend herself.

He doesn't blame Jensen. Not directly. But something lands — has Jensen even checked on her? Does he know what's happening to her online? Because Chace has seen it and Kelsey has seen it and Keleigh is still furious about the leaked photo from her own house and the person actually at the centre of it is sitting in the Canyon house alone while Jensen is in Connecticut doing school runs. The December sighting turned the fan communities from speculation into conviction and she's absorbing it with nobody in her corner who can actually correct the record.

Jensen doesn't have an answer. He hasn't called. He ended it. He doesn't think he has the right.

The Melton thing comes up. Chace was at the Miles and Keleigh party. The whole thing is fabricated and he's irritated on principle. Says something about how the press builds a relationship out of two photos of people standing near each other.

Jensen's face changes. He manages it but not fast enough. Chace sees it — the reaction of a man who has been looking at those photos for a week and drawing conclusions he couldn't verify.

Chace looks at him. Direct. Says it plainly — it's not true. None of it.

Jensen's relief is visible. The specific quality of a held breath releasing.

Chace watches the relief settle. Reads what it means — this man has been sitting alone in Connecticut looking at photos of Aoibhéann with another man and couldn't pick up the phone because she ended it. Chace saw them at the festival. Saw how Jensen looked at her across two days. Saw what it was.

*You know you really fucked that up.*

Not angry. Not a lecture. Just what he sees. Flat, factual, almost casual. And underneath it — the specific frustration of a friend who has watched her go through this before and is watching it happen again with a man who won't even pick up the phone.

Jensen doesn't argue. Doesn't explain. Just hears it. Chace just told him three things — she's not with anyone, she's being destroyed online, and he lost her.

### Beat 7 — JDM
*His POV — early Nov, before Chicago*

JDM. In person — he drives down from Rhinebeck or they meet somewhere. The way their conversations about this have always happened — face to face, private, JDM asking the right questions.

Jensen tells him. The break. What he said — that what he was offering wasn't enough. What she said — *you're right. It isn't enough.* The Misha conversation at Vancouver that prompted him to go to Danneel again. Danneel refusing. Telling Aoibhéann the result but not the attempt. Chace at NYCC — the fan backlash, the Melton photos, *you really fucked that up.*

JDM listens. Sits with it. Then asks the first question.

*Did you tell her it's because you love her? Did you tell her you were trying to protect her — or did you just tell her it wasn't enough?*

Jensen replays it. What he actually said. That he could see what it was costing her. That she deserved more than what he was offering. Everything around the word. Never the word.

JDM doesn't push. Just lets him hear what he said versus what he meant. The gap sitting in the room.

Then the second question. Not about the kids — that conversation already happened. Danneel already refused. The kids already know about the media. What JDM asks about is the house.

He tells Jensen about the twins. What Jensen told him — the family treated as intact a year after the decision. JDM asks him plainly — when is he moving out. Because the twins didn't forget. The twins never absorbed it. Nothing in their daily life changed. He still makes breakfast. He still does the school run. He's still in the house every morning and every evening. The word "separated" was said in January and nothing that followed made it real.

He can't fix Danneel. He can't force her to tell the kids about Aoibhéann. But he can stop living in a house where his children think their parents are still together. That's not Danneel's decision. That's his. And every day he stays is another day the gap between what they believe and what's true gets wider.

Jensen hears it. Knows it's true. Also knows what moving out means — not waking the kids up in the morning. Not being there when they go to bed. Sleeping somewhere else while their lives continue in the house he built for them.

JDM doesn't soften it. Says something about how doing the right thing for his kids and doing the comfortable thing for himself are two different things and he's been choosing the comfortable one for a year.

JDM doesn't tell him to call her. Doesn't tell him what to do about Aoibhéann. Just leaves him with the move-out question and the knowledge that the protection he built for his children is the same structure that made it impossible for her to exist in his life.

His team is arriving at the same reckoning from the other side. They need his actual future intentions clarified. If the long-term plan is temporary silence now and eventual real relationship later — then his personal decisions have to start matching that future path. His team stops thinking only in terms of suppression and starts thinking about what the eventual confirmation path looks like and when it can happen without detonating everything again.

*Dual POV — Nov 9–16*

Her — the Hailey Bieber meeting. Lunch or dinner, somewhere private. Not a pitch — a conversation. Aoibhéann asking the real questions. How did you start. What surprised you. What nearly killed it. What did it cost your other relationships.

The assumption that it was vanity rather than business. Aoibhéann doesn't have to imagine any of it. The fan communities spent October doing exactly this to her — building a version of her from photos and dates and a filing, then judging the version they built. She's sitting across from someone who launched a business inside that same machinery and survived it. That's not hypothetical anymore. It's the most relevant conversation she could be having.

She leaves with the beginning of a real plan. Skincare-infused makeup. The clean girl aesthetic she's been living for years turned into product. She knows the formulation world from the inside — fifteen years of being the face means fifteen years of understanding what's in the products. She knows what works. She knows what the industry charges for things that cost nothing to make. She knows the gap.

She doesn't tell Armani. Doesn't tell her father. Tells Cassidy to keep the meetings off the public calendar.

Him — after Chicago. Home. The JDM questions still sitting with him. Chace's face when he said *you really fucked that up.* Chace telling him what's happening to her online — the backlash he hadn't looked at because he was trying not to look at anything with her name on it. He probably doesn't find it — it's obscure, the editions are scattered across rare book dealers in London and Delhi and Paris. He asks his PA to track it down. She finds a copy — a first edition of *Twenty Jataka Tales* by Noor Inayat Khan. He doesn't explain why this specific book. His PA doesn't ask. She's watched the last month from the professional side and has the full picture without anyone giving it to her.

The book is sourced. He has it sent.

### Beat 9 — November 17
*Dual POV*

Her birthday. LA. The day is what her birthdays are — Cameron, maybe Keleigh, a dinner, the people who show up. Light. Not a production. She's thirty-six and not in the mood to perform celebration.

Something arrives. A package. She opens it and finds a first edition of *Twenty Jataka Tales*. No note. Or just her name in his handwriting.

She holds it. A children's book about compassion and self-sacrifice, written by a woman who everyone underestimated, who turned out to be the last person standing. A book she told him about at midnight on a phone call months ago. Not the spy story — the woman underneath it. The fairy tales she wrote before the war took her.

He remembered the specific thing that mattered. Not Noor the agent. Noor the person. The detail she told him because she was tired and the conversation had wandered and she was talking too fast with the accent out.

She doesn't cry. She sits with it. The book in her hands. The knowledge that four weeks of silence weren't what she thought they were. He wasn't moving on. He was listening the whole time.

He calls that evening. She picks up. Something about the book — dry, their register. A way in that isn't heavy. They're in the rhythm before either of them has decided what this call is.

Then he says it. That he should have called weeks ago. That Chace told him what was happening online and he should have checked on her. That he didn't because he thought she didn't want to hear from him — but that's not an excuse.

He hears what she's not saying. That it wasn't nothing four weeks ago. That she handled it alone.

The call settles back into their register. Not about anything heavy — what she did today, something about the book, a dry exchange that makes her laugh. The ease returning. The specific comfort of his voice after weeks without it.

Then he asks. He's in Austin this weekend. He wants her to come. They need to talk and not on the phone.

She goes quiet. Austin. His territory. The place he's from. A convention weekend — the thing that has pulled him away from her for months. He's asking her to come into that world.

She tells him to give her a day to think about it.

The call ends. She sits with the book and the request and the fact that for four weeks she told herself she was done and the sound of his voice just undid a month of work.

### Beat 10 — November 18
*Dual POV*

She calls the next day. Doesn't start warm. Doesn't start in their register. Starts with the question.

Why should she come. What has changed in the last four weeks. Why should she rearrange her plans last minute and fly to Texas. That's exactly the kind of thing he told her he couldn't keep doing to her. That's exactly why he ended it — because she was always the one adjusting, always the one fitting around his life. And now he wants her to do it again.

He hears it. She's right. Every word of it is what he said to her on that call. He used her own sacrifice as the reason to let her go and now he's asking her to sacrifice again. He has to answer that.

He tells her. Not defensively. Not managing it. Just honestly. The architecture of the last month.

Misha at Vancouver — showing him that three forces were deciding the shape of his relationship and none of them were about whether he and Aoibhéann wanted to be together. Going back to Danneel specifically because of what Misha said. Asking her again about the kids. Being refused again.

JDM driving down. A conversation that made it clear he never gave her the full picture of why he said what he said. That he handled it wrong. That the people closest to him aren't letting him off the hook either.

Chace at NYCC. Telling him the Melton rumours aren't true. Telling him what's happening to her online. Telling him he fucked up.

He's showing her that the people around him aren't letting him off the hook either. That this isn't just him sitting alone in Connecticut deciding he wants her back. Multiple people looked at what he did and told him he got it wrong.

She listens. The omission corrected — she didn't know he went to Danneel twice. Didn't know he tried. For four weeks she thought he reported the weather. He was actually in it.

She pushes back. That doesn't change the situation. Danneel still won't move. The kids still don't know. He's still in the house. What is actually different.

He says he knows. He can't promise the situation has changed. But the last time they talked he said the wrong thing. He told her what he was offering wasn't enough when what he should have said was something else entirely. And he'd like to say it to her face.

Then he drops the managed register entirely.

*And because I miss you. And I need to see you. There are things I have to say to you in person.*

The accent is there. The Texas surfacing the way it does when the management drops. That's not a position. Not a strategy. Just true.

She's quiet for a beat. Because she can push back on the Danneel situation. She can have feelings about the omission. She can be angry he didn't tell her he tried. But *I miss you and I need to see you* — she can't argue with that. And she knows it's true because she's been missing him for four weeks and pretending she isn't.

Okay. She'll come.

### Beat 1 — Austin, Friday
*Dual POV — Nov 20*

She lands at 8am. The flight from LA — booked herself, paid herself, the hotel booked herself. Hotel Saint Cecilia, South Congress, a bungalow. Private grounds, walled off. Her own room in his city. The Alex lesson built into every logistical detail — she will never again be in a city that isn't hers without her own way out.

Austin in November. Warmer than she expected. She doesn't leave the bungalow — thousands of his fans in the same city, all with phones, all actively following his personal life. One person spots her and the fan communities connect the dots within the hour. She sleeps. She works — Cassidy sends things, the beauty line research, emails. She reads. Room service. A woman who has spent fifteen years being professional in hotel rooms in cities that aren't hers. She knows how to do this.

### Beat 2 — The Conversation
*Dual POV — Friday evening*

She opens the door and five weeks collapses in a second. He's there. The physical reality of him after weeks of silence and phone calls. She feels it hit her — the specific way her body responds to his proximity before her head catches up.

That's exactly why she goes direct. Because if she lets the ease in first — the humour, the rhythm, the thing that always opens the door — she loses the ground she needs to stand on. She came here with questions and she's going to ask them before the rest of her overrides the part that needs answers.

She asks him straight. What does he have to say that required her to get on a plane.

He wasn't expecting it that fast. He had a version of this evening — they'd talk, find their way in, the conversation would open gradually. She just skipped every step and put him on the spot.

He starts with what he got wrong. Not the word — the mistake. He tells her what he should have said on that call. When he told her what he was offering wasn't enough, he meant it as protection. He could see what it was costing her and he thought the right thing to do was let her go before it cost more. He thought he was being honest. JDM made him see he was being a coward and calling it kindness.

She listens. This is what she came for — not a declaration but an explanation.

He tells her he knows saying what he's about to say doesn't fix the situation. Danneel hasn't moved. He's still in the house. The kids still don't know about her. The practical reality she called insufficient is still insufficient. She was right about all of it.

But she was wrong about one thing. She said love isn't enough. And he let her believe that was true by never telling her it was love. He let her build her case on evidence that was missing the one piece that changes what everything else means. Not because it fixes the logistics. Because it tells her what she's actually choosing between — not a situation that isn't enough, but a man who loves her inside a situation that isn't enough. Those are different decisions.

*I love you. I should have said it on that call. I should have said it before that. I've known for months and I managed it instead of saying it.*
