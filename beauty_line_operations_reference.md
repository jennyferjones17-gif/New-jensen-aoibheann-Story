## Core Product

**Format:** Hybrid skin-tint. Skincare-infused makeup. Masstige positioning.

**Hero product:** The hybrid skin-tint. Clean-beauty actives and botanicals (California lab territory) engineered with performance pigment and colour (New Jersey lab territory). Barrier-repairing, breathable, LA-glow-meets-NY-endurance.

**Custom packaging:** Airless pump. 15,000 custom units, overseas supplier. Airless design = no waste, stable formulation, no air exposure. Primary componentry is silk-screened or engraved with finalised logo as vector file.

**Target customer:** The no-makeup-makeup buyer. Skincare-as-self-care consumer. The woman who does pilates and wants to look like she's wearing nothing while her skin looks perfect. Alo Yoga's audience — "off duty model" in Alo marketing language. SKIMS customers already have beauty brand loyalty (Kim, Kylie). Alo's customer doesn't have an obvious beauty brand equivalent yet. That's the gap.

**Market position:** Sits next to Armani Beauty and Westman Atelier in masstige heritage-performance. NOT Rhode / Augustinus Bader / Goop clean-beauty-wellness register.

## Brand Name Lock

### Hard Deadline

Mid-to-late November. The exact moment she signs Purchase Orders with VC money. Name does NOT go public until January waitlist landing page — but the operational decision is immovable.

### Why Mid-to-Late November Is Immovable

**1. Packaging Bottleneck (primary & secondary componentry).** When she wires the deposit to the overseas supplier for 15,000 custom airless pumps, she isn't ordering blank plastic.
- **Silk-screening:** Factory needs the finalised logo as a vector file to physically print or engrave the brand name onto the bottles (primary componentry).
- **Unit cartons:** Cardboard secondary packaging requires the brand name, specific shade names, and ingredient list for legal compliance.
- If she delays the name, the factory cannot print. Instantly kills the late February NYFW launch timeline.

**2. Trademark Clearance (USPTO Class 3 — Cosmetics).** Before she spends VC money printing 15,000 boxes, attorney runs a USPTO clearance search on Class 3. She needs to know if a random indie brand in another state already owns the trademark. Filing in November protects her legally before a single bottle rolls off assembly.

**3. Digital Real Estate.** The moment the name is decided, her proxy silently buys the .com and secures Instagram/TikTok handles. Beauty industry is notorious for domain squatting. If a rumour leaks that Aoibhéann Connolly is starting a company called "ALTA," someone buys altabeauty.com and ransoms it for $50,000. November lock keeps the launch safe.

### Stealth Holding Company vs Brand Name

**Distinction matters.**
- **Stealth LLC** (e.g. "Bicoastal Ventures LLC") — legal shield. Keeps her name off public corporate registries during Phase 2 PR lockdown.
- **Brand name** — separately trademarked, printed on product, the commercial identity.
- VCs fund the LLC. Consumers buy the brand.

## Funding

### The VC Pressure Point: The Divorce

VC meetings go well on product. Hybrid concept is strong. She is the brand — her face, her audience, her credibility. Hero product makes sense. Market gap is real.

Then due diligence. Investors know about the rumours. Sundance coverage still indexed. Fan investigation searchable. Divorce public record — filed September 2026, not finalised. Over a year later, still not done.

### What Investors Actually Ask (in institutional language)

- *"We love the formulation, but we'd like to see some early traction first."* → Translation: not funding the launch while the tabloid fire is burning. Launch D2C yourself. If the internet boycotts the brand, it's your money lost, not ours.
- *"Let's stay close. We want to see how the market reacts to your transition from talent to CEO."* → Translation: if legacy brands drop you because of the married man association, your founder value plummets.
- *"We feel the timing just isn't right for our current fund deployment."* → Translation: the June finalisation target was missed, Danneel controls the timeline, they don't invest in indefinite PR liabilities.

### Underlying VC Fears

For a tech startup, personal drama rarely matters. For a founder-led masstige beauty brand, her face and reputation ARE the primary customer acquisition channels.

1. **The "Cancel" Premium:** If they fund inventory and marketing, and the divorce turns hostile at launch, the brand faces organised social media boycott. VCs don't want capital tied up in dead inventory in a California warehouse.
2. **Founder Distraction:** VCs want founders working 90-hour weeks on supply chains and retail pitches. Trapped in defensive PR posture and emotionally drained by a legal fight she can't control = too distracted to scale.

### Two Offers — Early November

**Option 1 — The Product Play** (arrives first):
- Lower total money. Available now — lands when Armani expires (end of November).
- They love the product: bicoastal supply chain, proprietary hybrid formulas from California and New Jersey labs, profit margins of custom airless pump packaging.
- But the marketing strategy is product-led — they don't want her face. She's stripped from the brand.
- Higher marketing costs because she can't leverage her following. Less money and more expensive to run.
- She hesitates — it's not what she wants, but it's real money on the table.

(Between arrivals: she has two pitches remaining. The first of those declines. One left.)

**Option 2 — The Founder Play, Tranched** (arrives Nov 13, day before she flies to Toronto):
- More money total.
- Her original strategy — founder-led, her face, her following, the conversion pipeline.
- Less marketing cost.
- But tranched.

### What Secures Option 2

Formula and airless pump lock — late October (pre-break beat, LA). Product validation is the anchor. VC commits because the product is real, the manufacturing timeline is real, and she's proven the founder. **NOT conditional on divorce finalisation.**

### Tranche Structure

**First tranche: less than 1/3 of total Option 2 money.** Lands when she accepts Option 2 in November — wired immediately on term sheet execution to cover manufacturing deposits.
- Covers manufacturing only
- Does NOT cover air-freight premium for 15,000 custom airless pumps
- Does NOT cover marketing / shoots / website design
- All of that is her own money

**Second tranche: $3M.** Unlocks after she proves revenue.
- $500K revenue target from D2C launch
- Expected March/April (Part Seven) after NYFW-seeded February launch drives awards-season MUA usage and organic conversion

### She Chooses Option 2

Decision made during November Toronto visit (birthday week). After: lawyer calls, Jack's advice (tranche deals are his world — entertainment financing), processing with Jensen (not asking him to decide, thinking out loud), processing with Cameron.

Jack sees through the conditional structure, knows it connects to Jensen, says something. She responds: she knows, it's not something she can or wants to change. Wants his business advice. He gives good advice. Then offers money. She's initially upset. After talking to Declan — reframed as professional respect. Jack doesn't throw money sentimentally. If he's offering, he assessed the business and decided it's viable. She still says no.

Between Jack's advice and belief, and Jensen showing he trusts her judgment — she takes Option 2. Confidence, not desperation.
