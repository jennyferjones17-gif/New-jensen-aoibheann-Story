### Beat 1 — Cameron's House, July 13 Evening
*Her POV*

The reader learns what happened that morning through Aoibhéann telling Cameron. The photos dropping while they were still together. Both phones. Her team wanting her out immediately. How she told Jensen to call Danneel, think of the kids before she thought about herself. Leaving before they could talk. Both too shocked. Her with her PR team after. Him with his. Him flying home. How all of it felt — the private thing torn open before they had a chance.

How she feels about Jensen. The connection she's starting to trust but not fully. How he actually sees her — for her. Not the model, not the name. But she can't fully trust it's real. She's spent years being seen for her looks and social standing, so she can't fully trust anyone. The people she thought were different — Cosmo, Alex, Garrett — weren't different. Or at least she didn't think they were.

Her team's strategy. Deny, distance, charity content. Being told to act like it's nothing.

His team's strategy. Hold him to silence. No emotional public reaction. No defensive language. No attempt to "clear up" what happened. The internal objective is simple: stop this becoming a love story in public.

His kids. She was the child on the outside of her father's other life. She can't do that to his children.

They haven't talked. Jensen hasn't called.

Cameron's response — she tells Aoibhéann she saw how he looks at her. How he gravitated toward her all weekend. Yes there is a good chance his feelings are real. But is that enough?

You haven't called him either.

Cameron asks if she's told Jensen any of this — the connection, the fear, the history, what she wants.

No.

The argument: Cameron gives her examples — Garrett. RJ. People she never let in, never allowed anything to actually develop with. Aoibhéann argues back — they were different, she never felt the connection with them. Cameron — because she didn't let it develop. Aoibhéann — she did with Cosmo and that turned out just as bad. Cameron — because Aoibhéann convinced herself he was like Alex when he wasn't. Aoibhéann — she doesn't know that for sure, doesn't know he didn't cheat. Cameron — *you know he didn't.*

After the argument settles: Aoibhéann sitting with it. Considering that she might be holding Jensen at arm's length. Whether that's right or wrong. Maybe she should trust this. If he calls, maybe she really can trust it.

Cameron is more worried than before but doesn't say it. She can see Aoibhéann's feelings are deep — deeper than she expected. But she can also see every complication a relationship with Jensen would bring and isn't sure Aoibhéann will be able to cope with them. Jensen or the situation could really hurt her. And by the time they both realise it won't work, Aoibhéann will have wasted more time on the wrong person, be damaged further, be too old to start over with someone else. Cameron keeps that to herself. For now.

The July photos have spiked harder online than April did. But the chatter is immediately complicated by her holiday content and the men around her on that trip. Fan forums split between "something happened with Jensen at the event" and "she's obviously onto someone from the holiday already." The photos from the event and the photos from St-Tropez running side by side in the same threads — the ambiguity keeping it from hardening into certainty.

Both teams monitoring closely. Neither intervenes. His team: do not interfere while the dominant reading stays rebound, brief hook-up, not serious. That narrative is ugly but useful — far safer than ongoing secret relationship, emotional betrayal, family disruption for a serious partner. Her team: do not intervene. Acceptable narratives: rebound, hook-up, not serious. Prevents "serious relationship" framing.

### Beat 5 — The Second Text and the Second Call
*Dual POV — July 15/16*

Later that day or in the middle of the night. He texts again. Tells her he hoped the call would have gone differently. Can they try again and not talk PR strategies.

She texts back — she has work commitments the next day but she's free after 3pm her time. Her text doesn't give much away. He doesn't know what she expects from the call. She's not sure either but maybe a little hopeful.

When he calls — awkward at first. Then they both laugh because they're being awkward. It eases the tension.

He asks her plainly — why did you leave so quickly without saying anything.

She doesn't know what there was to say.

He almost blurts out that he wanted her to say this is something. That he didn't imagine this.

She's stunned. Silent. Then asks if he wants this to be something.

He says of course he does. But he needs to know she's in this before he upsets his whole family and social dynamic.

She's honest — or thinks she's honest — and says she doesn't know. Not because she doesn't know how she feels about him. She doesn't know if feelings, their connection, is enough. She's learned from her parents and all her significant relationships that feelings and connection are not enough to sustain anything.

He pushes back — it's enough if you want it to be.

She counters — she's been proven right her whole life. If he thinks love is enough then they're fundamentally different and their lives are fundamentally different. She knows in her world, her family, love is not enough and it's the thing that can destroy you. So maybe he's simply too good for her.

He doesn't know how to take that. Can't understand how she can think that.

She's shocked by everything she just revealed. Says she has to go.

After the call he replays it over and over. At some point he realises — she said love. That love isn't enough. But he heard feelings, connection and love. His doubts about this being one-sided are starting to disappear completely. But he doesn't know how to convince her or whether trying to would further damage what's between them.
