## CONNECTICUT DIVORCE / CUSTODY LAW

### Key Principle
- Divorce can proceed in CT even without a fixed, formal custody schedule.
- CT courts require child arrangements are addressed before divorce is FINALISED — but NOT necessarily a rigid 50/50 schedule.

### What Is Required Before Finalisation
- Either: parents submit a Parenting Plan (where children primarily live, how time is shared, decision-making) — OR court decides based on "best interests of the child."

### Current Setup (legally strong)
- Dad has daily involvement (school runs, meals, evenings). Kids primarily in one home. No overnights yet. High cooperation.
- This can be formalised as: primary residence with one parent + flexible parenting time with the other.
- Courts care about actual involvement, not just overnights. Dad's level of involvement is very strong legally.

### What Lawyers/Mediators Recommend
- Create a temporary or flexible parenting plan. Not a rigid custody schedule.
- Example: children primarily reside with one parent. Father has daily contact, agreed parenting time (loosely defined). Overnights: "to be introduced by agreement when appropriate."
- This is completely acceptable to the court.

### Why Courts Don't Force Structure Too Early
- CT uses "best interests of the child" standard. Won't push for overnights or rigid schedules if kids are still adjusting, arrangements working, parents cooperative.

### When More Formal Schedule Becomes Necessary
- When conflict arises, one parent pulls back access, or ambiguity exists. Not their current situation.

### Danneel's Suggestion (Ch5 B11)
- If they're talking about finalising finances, they should also speak to the counsellor about what comes next and how to prepare the kids. This connects to the parenting plan — not rigid, reflecting current reality.
