- Acknowledging without dismissing; giving kids control of what they decide about Aoibhéann

## KIDS INTRODUCTION — FULL FRAMEWORK

### Before She Arrives
- Dad arrives with kids first. Already seated or just ordered. Kids settled.
- She arrives separately — feels like a light overlap, not a takeover.

### Her Arrival
- Calm, not rushing, not overly smiley.
- Dad's intro: just her name. Nothing else. No framing.
- She says: "Hi, nice to meet you." Short. Not "I've heard so much about you."
- Sits next to Dad, not opposite kids (avoids interview setup).

### First Minutes
- JJ: brief eye contact, nod, withdraws to observation mode.
- Zeppelin: looks directly, more open, says hi.
- Arrow: stays close to Dad, limited eye contact, hands occupied, watching.

### Conversation Flow
- Dad leads lightly. Normal topics, not about the situation.
- She follows, doesn't lead. Speaks when it naturally opens.

### What Kids Ask
- Zeppelin's interests are soccer and baseball. He connects through sport — Dodger Stadium event, soccer players from her holiday photos. Kids have looked her up online.
- JJ: asked "Is she your girlfriend?" to Jensen (not Aoibhéann). He said yes. She already knew — confirming it to his face.
- Arrow: doesn't speak during the meeting.

### Her Answers
- Short, neutral, no expansion. Don't build on it unless the child pulls more.

### Critical Rules
- No physical affection with Jensen in front of kids.
- Jensen stays focused on kids, doesn't shift attention to her.
- Don't ask kids personal questions. Don't try to connect. Don't try to be funny or impressive.
- End before it gets awkward. 45 minutes.
- She leaves separately. No hug. No lingering.
- Do NOT debrief kids immediately. No "what did you think?"

### Success =
- No visible discomfort, no withdrawal, no tension.
- NOT laughter, bonding, or connection.
- Feels like something small that happened, not something important.

## KIDS AT HIS HOUSE — SUMMER VISITS FRAMEWORK

### The Correct Framing
- This summer is about making Dad's place feel familiar and safe — WITHOUT pressure.
- NOT about creating a second home or splitting time.
- Comfort, not transition.

### Phase 1 (early summer, after introduction)
- Short, casual visits. 1–2 hours. Drop-ins, not planned "stays."
- Stopping by briefly, picking something up, having a drink/snack, sitting for a bit, then leaving.
- The feeling: "We go there sometimes" NOT "We stay there now."

### Phase 2 (mid-summer, if stable)
- Slightly longer visits (2–3 hours). Maybe early evening time.
- Still: no overnights, no structured "you're staying here today."

### What NOT To Do This Summer
- No overnight stays.
- No "this is your room now" setup.
- No fixed schedule like "you're with Dad here on these days."
- This creates a second-home expectation before they're ready.

### Key Principle
- Frequency > duration. Five short visits better than one long "stay."
- Building familiarity without expectation.

### How Each Child Responds
- JJ (14): notices everything, tests whether anything feels "off." Short visits = safe. Structured stays = pressure.
- Zeppelin (9, curious): explores, asks questions, engages. Warms naturally with repeated short visits.
- Arrow (9, quiet): observes, needs repetition to feel comfortable. Multiple short exposures work best.

### Before Dad Leaves (Aug 16)
- One slightly longer visit is fine (but still not overnight).
- Goal: "We know that place, it's fine" NOT "That's where Dad lives now and we don't."

### When Overnights Happen
- NOT this summer. Later — after Dad returns from filming. By then, if short visits went well, overnights feel natural, not imposed.

## KIDS — BLUNT QUESTIONS FRAMEWORK

### Common Questions + Correct Responses
1. "Is she your girlfriend?" → "Yes." (Then stop. No explanation.) — ALREADY HAPPENED (Ch5 B5)
2. "Is she why you moved out?" → "No." Then: "That was about things between me and your other parent." Then move on.
3. "Do you love her?" → "I care about her, yes." Do NOT escalate emotionally.
4. "Is she going to live with you?" → "No." Or: "Not something we're thinking about."
5. "This is weird." → "Yeah — it probably does feel a bit different." Then stop. Validate without fixing.
6. "I don't like this." → "Okay. You don't have to decide anything right now." Then move on.

### Rules
- Don't correct them sharply. Don't say "that's rude." Don't over-explain. Don't defend her.
- She should stay quiet during blunt moments — this is a Dad-kids conversation, not hers to manage.
- Kids are testing how safe it is to say what they think, not testing the answer itself.

### Arrow — "Why Do People Say She's Bad?" (expected later)
- Most likely to bring this up in a quieter, one-on-one moment. NOT during a group meeting.
- Correct response: "Some people say things when they don't know the full story." "My experience of her is different." "You don't have to decide anything about her right now." Then stop.
- Acknowledges what she heard. Doesn't dismiss her concern. Doesn't over-defend Aoibhéann. Gives her control.
