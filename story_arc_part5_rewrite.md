## Part Five Operating Principle

She says yes to the trial. But every week demands new decisions — from her, from him, from Danneel, from both of them together. The gates don't open on schedule. They open because someone chooses to push, concede, or force. His world is finally starting to open, and she needs what's inside it before she can decide whether to stay. But the information doesn't arrive neatly. It arrives in the middle of decisions that can't wait for it.

Timeline: May through August 2027.

## Chapter 1 — The Answer
*Late April – May 7*
*POV: Primarily Aoibhéann. Jensen POV in Beat 5 (Jared conversation).*

### Beat 1 — Cassidy / The Canyon House
*Her POV — Canyon house, late April, work morning*

The Canyon house. A day or two after Cameron's. Cassidy at the dining table, laptop open, the usual morning rhythm — schedule, emails, upcoming commitments.

Aoibhéann raises it. She's considering spending more time in New York. Doesn't frame it as a decision — frames it as something she's thinking about. Doesn't mention Jensen.

Cassidy doesn't ask why.

They go through it practically. The Armani contract — the 48-hour clause for unplanned LA shoots. She'd need to be able to get back within two days of any call. That means flights booked and flexibility built into whatever her NYC schedule looks like. Cassidy would work remotely for the days Aoibhéann is east, manage the house, coordinate LA logistics from here.

Cassidy raises the beauty line — the lawyers are in New York, the consultants are in New York. The line doesn't have a home yet. Being in NYC more regularly could actually move that forward.

They go through the rest. LA shoots she's locked into — Max Mara next week. The schedule after that lighter through May. Cannes mid-month, which is neither coast.

Cassidy asks if JFK is the preferred airport for flights back.

Aoibhéann says she doesn't know yet. She's just thinking about it.

The beat ends with them back on the regular schedule. The conversation absorbed into the working day. The house around them — the one she bought after Cosmo, the one she planned to stay in — discussed as something that would need looking after in her absence.

### Beat 2 — The Call / I Don't Know Yet
*Dual POV — phone call, evening his time, late April*

Evening call. Their usual rhythm. He asks about her day — she tells him about Max Mara next week, Cannes logistics starting. He tells her about the kids. Work — development, scripts, what Chaos Machine has in the pipeline.

She tells him she hasn't decided yet. About the East Coast. She's thinking about it but she doesn't have an answer.

He says okay. That she should take whatever time she needs. Moves to something about the agency meetings in May — scheduling, who he's sitting down with.

She asks if he's really okay with that.

He's quiet for a beat. Then honest. He says he hoped she'd have an answer. Not because he's pressuring her — because the last month made the waiting harder. They've seen each other almost every couple of weeks since February. The five days at Larchmont. Cooking in that kitchen, the no-alarm Saturday morning, driving back and forth from Fairfield. And then going back to the phone. To the schedule. To planning the next window.

He says it's harder to go back to the distance when he knows what the alternative feels like.

She doesn't fill it with reassurance. Holds the space. Says she knows. That it's harder for her too.

The call continues. Lighter after — he tells her something about the twins, she tells him something about Cassidy. The usual register returns. But what he said sits with her after they hang up.

### Beat 3 — Declan
*Her POV — Saturday May 1, surfing*

The Saturday they agreed to. The break is good. They're in the water before she brings it up.

After. She tells him she's thinking about spending more time in New York. A trial. Not permanent. Jack's apartment first, then maybe a short-term rental.

Declan says he knew this was coming.

She asks what he means.

He says he's known since it got serious. That this is how it works — Jensen has kids, the kids are in Connecticut, the kids can't move. Only one person can.

She tells him it's a trial. That she's not leaving LA. That the house isn't going anywhere.

He says could you not have found someone who at least lives in LA.

She laughs. He's half laughing. The thing underneath it is not a joke.

She tells him about the call with Jensen — that he admitted the distance is getting harder. That the five days showed them both what it could look like and going back to the phone after that is worse than before.

Declan asks how much time she's talking about.

She doesn't know yet. A week at a time, maybe more. Depends on work. Armani has the 48-hour clause for unplanned shoots but that contract expires by the end of the year and she's not renewing it. Everything else is short-term — no fixed base.

He's quiet for a bit. Then says the thing about the drive from his place to hers — twenty-five minutes. That he likes knowing she's twenty-five minutes away.

She hears it. Doesn't fix it. Says she's not disappearing. Says nothing changes between them.

He says okay. Doesn't push further. They talk about something else — his job, the surf, something ordinary.

What he said sits with her alongside what Jensen said about the five days. Two people she loves pulling in opposite directions on the same question.

### Beat 4 — Cameron
*Her POV — May 1 or 2*

Cameron's house. Three days since the conversation that ended Part Four.

Aoibhéann tells her she's going to do it. A trial. NYC first — Jack's apartment, then a short-term rental if it works. Not Westchester, not Connecticut. Not permanent.

Cameron asks if she's sure.

Aoibhéann tells her about Jensen's call — that he admitted the last month made it harder, the five days at Larchmont, going back to the distance after knowing what the alternative feels like. And Declan — that he knew this was coming, the twenty-five minutes line.

Cameron says she needs to make sure she gives the trial real time. Actually lives in it. Sees what the day-to-day looks like — him driving down from Connecticut, the 5am alarms, being in an apartment alone during the day while he's ninety minutes away with his kids. Not a week of playing house. Actual time.

And that before she makes any permanent decisions — Westchester, a second place, anything beyond the trial — the doubts she laid out on Tuesday need to be worked through. The women pattern. The career question. The kids question. Those didn't go away because she's saying yes to a trial. They're the reason the trial isn't permanent.

Aoibhéann says she knows. That's why it's NYC and not Westchester. That's why it's Jack's apartment and not a lease.

Cameron says okay. Then asks about the beauty line, about Cannes, about the practical shape of it. They talk through the schedule. Cameron in support mode — showing up, not challenging.

The answer is made. She knows what she's going to tell him.

### Beat 5 — The Answer
*Dual POV — phone call May 2 or 3, then Jensen/Jared a day later*

**Her call:**

Evening call. She tells him she's been thinking about it and talking to people. She wants to try it. More time in New York. A trial — to see if it actually gives them more time, if it works with her schedule.

He asks what she's thinking.

She says NYC. Jack's apartment first. Not Westchester, not Connecticut. Everyone she actually likes on the East Coast is in the city. She doesn't know if Westchester would feel isolated — her in a house in the suburbs while he's ninety minutes away. Jack's place she knows. She's been coming there her whole life. If it works, maybe a short-term rental after.

He says yeah. She can hear it in his voice — real, not performed. He asks about timing. She says Max Mara finishes the 6th. She could fly out after that, be in New York for a week before Cannes. Come back after Cannes too.

He asks about Armani — the 48-hour clause for LA shoots. She says Cassidy's on it. And the contract expires end of year. She's not renewing.

He says he knows. That this is a lot for her to rearrange.

She says she wants to try it.

The call is lighter after that. He tells her about the kids — something specific from the day. She tells him about Declan, about surfing Saturday. Doesn't tell him what Declan said about the twenty-five minutes.

**Jensen / Jared — a day later.**

Jensen calls Jared. Catches him up — he hasn't talked to him properly since late March. Tells him about Easter at her family's estate in Greenwich. The structured calls they did in April — taking turns asking hard questions. That he went to LA, then she came to New York for five days. That he asked her about spending more time on the East Coast. A second place maybe. Somewhere closer to him and the kids.

Jared asks what she said.

Jensen says she said yes. A trial. NYC. Her dad's apartment.

Jared asks how far that is from Fairfield.

Jensen says ninety minutes. Hour and a half each way.

Jared says that's still a lot of driving.

Jensen says he knows. That he was thinking Westchester — forty minutes, close enough for a weeknight dinner without it being a whole thing. But she said NYC. Said everyone she knows on the East Coast is in the city. That Westchester would be her alone.

Jared asks if she's right about that.

Jensen doesn't answer immediately. Then says yes. That his mornings are the kids. Breakfast, school run, every day. He'd drive down in the evening and leave at five. She'd be in the apartment alone most of the day.

Jared says she moved across the country for him. That's the headline.

Jensen says he knows. That he caught himself being disappointed and then heard how that sounds. She's leaving LA — her brother, her best friend, her house, her whole life. And his first thought was closer.

Jared says take the yes.

### Beat 7 — Max Mara / Keleigh
*Her POV — May 3–6, LA*

Max Mara shoot. Three days. The work rhythm — wardrobe, lighting, setups. Professional, efficient, the version of her job she can do without thinking.

Dinner with Keleigh after the shoot or during the week. Keleigh asks about summer. Mentions the villa — they're looking at the same place in St-Tropez, Nina's staying for a few days in July, trying to get the dates to work around everyone's schedule.

Aoibhéann didn't know about this. Keleigh sees it land. Catches herself — assumes Kelsey or someone mentioned it. They've been planning it for a few weeks.

Aoibhéann says she didn't know. That nobody asked. Keleigh is uncomfortable — says it wasn't deliberate, they just assumed she couldn't come. That last year the posting was strategic and this year everything is more complicated. They stopped asking because the answer was always going to be complicated.

Aoibhéann says it's fine. It is not fine. The thing she can hear: they planned around her assumed unavailability. They stopped including her because including her requires planning around the PR situation. It was easier not to ask.

Keleigh asks about the charity — she heard there's a gala dinner next month and asks why Aoibhéann didn't mention it. Whether she's organising something.

Aoibhéann tells her. She stepped back. After the December dinner and January dinner came out publicly, they lost sponsors. Companies that had committed pulled out. The association with the relationship narrative made the charity a liability for their own brands. Richard and Paige are running it now. She's still involved behind the scenes but her name is off the front.

Keleigh didn't know. Asks how many sponsors.

Aoibhéann tells her. Enough that the event she spent a year building can't run the same way twice.

Keleigh sits with it.

Aoibhéann tells her about NYC. That she's going to spend more time there. A trial. Jack's apartment first.

Keleigh isn't surprised. But asks if this is the right time — Armani still active, the beauty line in development, every brand relationship she has is LA-based. And New York is close enough to Connecticut that everyone will work it out. If she's suddenly spending weeks in NYC, the people already watching will connect it.

Keleigh also says that if she's suddenly in New York regularly, people in the industry will notice. NYC is where the money is — investors, beauty conglomerates, finance. People will assume she's taking meetings. If anyone connects that to a potential launch while Armani is still active, the exclusivity clause becomes a problem before she's ready.

Aoibhéann says the beauty line lawyers and consultants are already in New York. That's not new. But Keleigh's right that her being there more often changes how it looks.

Aoibhéann says she knows. That she's thought about all of it.

Keleigh says she hopes it works. Means it.

### Beat 8 — Shaun
*Her POV — May 5 or 6, in person, LA*

She asks Shaun to meet. He says yes. In person — his place or hers or somewhere they used to go.

She asks him directly. What happened. Between them. Since Sundance.

He doesn't dodge it. Tells her.

The PR thing first. They can't even have lunch. It doesn't matter where they go — someone's taking a photo. Every restaurant, every coffee place, even in a group. People with phones watching, waiting. It's not just that she's restricted. It's that being around her now means being in the surveillance. His privacy invaded because of her situation.

Then that she pulled away. That he'd suggest meeting up and she'd say it had to be at her house or in a group. That the invitations got complicated and then they just stopped.

Then Jensen. That every second conversation she has is about Jensen. The logistics, the PR, the schedule, the distance. That the person he used to talk to about everything — surfing, life, nothing — is gone. Replaced by someone managing a situation.

Then Sundance. What she said about RJ. The group conversation after the Cosmo thing — why Jensen, why men outside her world. RJ was in that room. RJ is Shaun's close friend. A man who was genuinely into her and she closed the door on him. Hearing her explain her pattern with relationships while RJ sat there.

Then the thing that lands hardest. He says he feels like he doesn't know her anymore.

She's quiet.

She tells him she understands the PR part. That she hates it too. And that she knows she pulled away — that she didn't know how to have a casual friendship inside the architecture her life had become.

She tells him she's sorry about RJ. That she handled it badly and she knows that.

But him saying he doesn't know her anymore — she doesn't have a response for that. She hears it and it sits with her.

They don't fix it. The conversation ends without resolution. Not hostile — just honest. Two people who used to be easy together acknowledging that they aren't anymore.

She leaves knowing this friendship has changed shape. And that she's about to leave the city where it existed.

### Beat 1 — JFK / Jack's Apartment / The Roadmap
*Her POV — JFK arrivals then Jack's UES apartment, May 7*

May 7. JFK. Morning. Red eye landed.

She comes through arrivals and he's there. Not a car service. Him. She stops for a second, then walks to him.

She works it out quickly. He called Cassidy for the flight details. Cassidy didn't book her a car. She asks why Cassidy didn't tell her. He says he asked her not to.

She's genuinely happy. Not performing it — the kind of surprised that lands before she can manage it.

Her first question. She asks if the kids and Danneel are okay with him missing breakfast and the school run. He says yes. That he told Danneel, and it's part of what the counsellor advised — that the kids adjusting to flexibility in the routine now is better than protecting a pattern that's going to change anyway.

She asks what he means by that. He says the counsellor has a roadmap. He'll tell her at the apartment.

The drive to the Upper East Side. Morning traffic. They talk — her flight, the red eye, whether she slept. Light. The register of two people who haven't been in the same city for weeks and are adjusting to the physical fact of each other.

Jack's apartment. She lets them in. The prewar hallway, the gallery, the rooms she's known her whole life. Photos on the walls — presidents, Oscar winners, family, all displayed with the same casual authority. Updated in layers, not all at once. Some rooms more modern than others. Her room still recognisable as hers even though the furniture has been upgraded. The apartment has memory in it.

She puts her bags in her room. He knows the apartment — he's stayed here before, multiple times. But the context is different now. Not a visit. A trial.

She tells him Shannon is coming tomorrow. Staying the weekend, leaving Monday. She says Shannon doesn't know about the trial — why she's here. And won't be finding out. As far as Shannon is concerned she's in New York for work.

He asks if him staying Sunday night is a problem. She says the apartment is big enough. Then the dry version — they're in her childhood bedroom with her little sister a couple of doors down.

He laughs. She says at least there's no curfew.

They settle. Coffee in the kitchen. She asks about the roadmap.

He tells her. The counsellor has mapped it in phases.

Phase 1 is May. Stabilise. No introduction. His presence in the kids' lives consistent — breakfast, school run, the routine holding. The kids seeing that nothing is shifting underneath them. At the same time, he starts normalising Aoibhéann. Not introducing her — mentioning her. Her name in the house in ordinary ways. Something she said that was funny. Something she's working on. The goal is that by the time he tells them he's seeing someone, her name isn't new information. It's already attached to a person in their father's life, not just a name from the internet.

Phase 2 is late May or early June. He sits them down and tells them he's seeing someone. They already know her name — JJ from the internet, the twins from whatever JJ has said. But their version is the internet's version. This replaces it with his. Who she is. What she does. That she matters to him. The counsellor says the conversation should be factual, not emotional — not asking them to feel anything about it, just giving them information.

Phase 3 is June, before school breaks around the 21st. A first meeting. Short — an hour, maybe two. A neutral setting, not the family house, not a restaurant where it feels like a performance. No forced bonding, no family framing. The counsellor's word is observational — the kids observe her, she observes them, nobody is required to like anyone. If it goes well, it goes well. If someone is uncomfortable, that's information too.

Phase 4 is summer. Mostly separate. Parent-child time. His weeks with the kids are his weeks. Light integration only if Phase 3 went well and nobody is resisting.

She asks about JJ specifically. He says JJ pushed back when Danneel corrected Arrow's "bad person" comment. That JJ has formed her own view from what she's read and seen online. The counsellor says JJ needs her father's version before she meets the real person — that the meeting itself isn't the hard part. The hard part is JJ walking in having already decided, from information that isn't accurate, who this woman is.

She asks about the twins. He says they're easier in some ways — younger, less online. But Arrow repeated something she heard. The counsellor says that's normal at nine — absorbing language from the environment without understanding the weight of it. Doesn't mean it's fixed in her.

She's quiet for a moment. Then says she hopes the roadmap works. That she doesn't want them to feel about her the way she felt about Stephanie. That if there's a version of this where they meet her and it's just ordinary — no weight, no performance — that's what she wants.

He hears it. Doesn't push. Says the counsellor's whole approach is exactly that — ordinary, low-stakes, no pressure.

The evening. Her room, the apartment she's known since childhood. Tomorrow he leaves at 5am — JJ's 14th birthday. He won't come back Saturday night. Shannon arriving. Rob and Ashley dinner already arranged.

The trial has started.

### Beat 2 — JJ's Birthday / Shannon / Rob and Ashley
*Her POV — Jack's UES apartment then dinner, May 8*

May 8. Saturday. 5am alarm. He gets up. She's awake — doesn't pretend she isn't. He says he'll call her later. JJ's birthday. He drives to CT.

The apartment without him. She showers, makes coffee in Jack's kitchen. Works from the dining table — laptop, calls, the beauty line. She's at the decision point on positioning. Mass prestige like Rhode or Fenty — scale, accessibility, the numbers work faster. Or high-end luxury like Victoria Beckham — smaller, more controlled, but she'd need to find a production partner the way Beckham did. The lawyers and consultants are in New York. Being here puts her in the same city as the people she needs to make this decision with.

Shannon arrives mid-morning. Bags in her room — her room, down the corridor from Aoibhéann's. The apartment absorbing another Connolly without adjusting.

They overlap in the kitchen. Shannon asks how long she's in New York. Aoibhéann says a week or so, then Cannes. Shannon asks if Jensen is here too. Aoibhéann says he's in Connecticut — JJ's birthday.

Shannon says something about the whole arrangement. That she can't believe all of this is for a guy who's almost fifty. A genre TV actor. She doesn't say it with hostility — she says it the way someone repeats a line they've heard at the family table without examining it. The same language from the Christmas dinner. Stephanie's framing, Shannon's mouth.

Aoibhéann doesn't take it on. Says something short and dry that closes it. Shannon moves on — she has plans in the city tonight, friends or other cousins.

The afternoon. More beauty line work. He calls — JJ's birthday, the day, the kids. Light. She doesn't ask for detail about Danneel. He doesn't offer it.

Evening. Rob and Ashley. A restaurant Rob picked — somewhere they've been before. Ashley is friendly, present, still mainly Rob's girlfriend in the room rather than someone Aoibhéann knows independently.

Rob asks what she's doing in New York. He knows she's been planning to spend more time here. She tells him the rest. The trial. Jack's apartment first. Jensen driving down from CT, 5am alarms, the shape of it. That the counsellor has a roadmap for the kids.

The dinner is easy. The first social interaction of the trial that isn't about Jensen, isn't about the family, isn't about what she's giving up. Just a cousin she likes, in the city she's trying to live in.

She gets back to the apartment. Shannon's out. The place quiet. He texts — goodnight, JJ had a good birthday.

### Beat 3 — Shannon / Danneel / The Timeline Question
*Dual POV (weighted toward her) — Jack's UES apartment, May 9–10*

May 9. Sunday. He drives to NYC evening. Shannon is still in the apartment — leaving tomorrow morning.

Shannon meets Jensen properly for the first time inside the apartment. She's curious, direct, assessing. Asks him questions — about the kids, about Connecticut, about the arrangement. Refers to Danneel as his wife. Not hostile — technically correct. He corrects her. Says Danneel, or my ex.

Shannon does it again later. His wife. Aoibhéann clocks it but doesn't intervene the second time. Shannon asks something about Aoibhéann's dating history — light, but pointed. The kind of comment that frames Jensen as the latest in a series. Aoibhéann closes it the same way she closed the genre TV actor line — short, dry, done.

Jensen handles Shannon fine. Polite, doesn't rise to it. But after Shannon goes to her room, the apartment is quiet and the evening carries what Shannon left in it. Aoibhéann says something about Shannon being Stephanie's daughter — that the framing comes pre-installed.

They go to bed. Her childhood bedroom. Shannon down the corridor. The joke from Beat 1 still holding — but the apartment feels different with a Connolly witness in it.

May 10. Monday. 5am alarm. He drives to CT. Shannon leaves mid-morning — bags, goodbye, back to her life in the city. The apartment empties.

Her day alone. Beauty line work. The apartment without either of them in it.

He's at the family house. Breakfast, school run. Then he raises the divorce with Danneel.

She's at the rental. Beauty line calls — the NJ lab on pigment range, her lawyer on contract structure. Metro North into the city for a meeting. Back by mid-afternoon. The house quiet.

He texts her at nine-thirty. Long day. Kids wanted him to watch something after dinner. He stayed. He's at Sunnieholme now. Too tired to drive. She says okay. Goodnight.

Aug 12. Tuesday. Amazon meetings. Studio alignment. The franchise, the marketing, the tone. Long blocks on Zoom, one in-person meeting he drives to. Back for dinner at Sasco Hill. The twins ask about Toronto — when he's going, how long. He tells them. Zeppelin asks if they can visit. Jensen says maybe. Arrow asks if he's coming back for Thanksgiving. He says yes.

He comes to the rental after dinner. They sit on the porch. He tells her about the Amazon meetings, the scripts, what the season looks like. She can hear the shift happening — he's already half inside the work.

The filming period. He has his schedule — the free weekends mapped. They go through them against her calendar. Beauty line meetings, brand commitments, what can move and what can't. She'll fly out to him. They'll go somewhere outside Toronto for the weekend — not the city, not where the crew is. His idea. He says he can't go the full stretch to the mid-season break without seeing her. Too long.

She asks about the risk. He says they'll be careful. Says maybe they could also do something like the Path B dinner — people from the show she knows, a reason to be in the same city. They'll work it out once he's there.

He stays the night.

Aug 13. Wednesday. Scripts and character work. Solo read-through, arc discussions with the showrunner. The shift from producer to actor.

That evening at Sasco Hill. After dinner he's in the TV room with the kids. A film. Zeppelin on one side, Arrow on the other. JJ in the armchair. He falls asleep on the couch with the twins. Wakes up at midnight. The TV off — JJ turned it off and went to bed. Arrow asleep against his shoulder. Zeppelin sprawled across the other end. He stays.

Texts Aoibhéann from the couch. Fell asleep with the kids. Staying. She texts back — good.

Aug 14. Thursday. Practical prep. Costume approvals remotely, stunt calls, dialect work. The last day he can meaningfully influence things from CT.

That evening at Sasco Hill. The twins ask if they can camp in the garden. Sleeping bags, torches, the lawn. Arrow asks if he's staying too. He says yes.

He calls Aoibhéann from the garden while the twins set up. Tells her. She says of course. He can hear them arguing about torch placement.

He sleeps in the garden. Stars and the sound of Long Island Sound from across the road. Arrow falls asleep first. Zeppelin talks for another twenty minutes — Montana, the horses, whether Jensen has ever seen a bear. Then he's out.

Aug 15. Friday. Last influence day. One-on-ones with the showrunner and the first block director. Scenes that aren't working. After this his input becomes reactive.

That evening at Sasco Hill. Dinner with the kids. Last dinner before he leaves. They know the pattern — he's done this their whole lives. But this time he doesn't live there. The goodbye tomorrow is from a different house.

He comes to the rental after. Stays.

Aug 16. Saturday. He goes to Sunnieholme. Packing. Long-term stay — twenty weeks. The house will sit empty except for breaks. She drives over and helps him. Stands in his office while he decides what to take. The photos stay. The guitar goes.

They drive back to the rental together. Last night.

Aug 17. Sunday morning. He goes to Sasco Hill early. Breakfast with the kids. Tells them he'll call every day. Tells them he'll be back for breaks. JJ hugs him — brief, tight. Zeppelin says bring something back from Toronto. Arrow holds onto him and doesn't let go for a long time. Danneel in the kitchen. He says goodbye to her. She says good luck with the season. Means it.

He drives to the rental. She's waiting. They have an hour.

They don't talk about the buried wish or the LA conversation or the divorce counter. They talk about when they'll speak — every night, late, after the kids' call. The first free weekend. Where she'll be — NYC for a few days, then LA, then back to the rental when he's home for breaks.

He holds her. She lets him.

He leaves. She watches his car go down the court and turn onto Gregory Boulevard. The same view as the first morning at the rental. Different now.

The house. The harbour. Her sheets on the bed. The jumper she left at his house returned and folded on the kitchen counter. Grand Central forty minutes south.

She stands in the bedroom. Considers what to pack for New York.

Him at the airport. The guitar in its case. Toronto. Twenty weeks.

The trial built on proximity. The proximity is gone.

**Part Five ends.**
